# Atoms & the building blocks of matter

Everything you can touch is made of matter. Chemistry asks what that matter is made of, how it behaves, and how it changes.

## Inside an atom

An **atom** has a small, dense nucleus surrounded by electrons. The nucleus contains positively charged **protons** and, in most atoms, uncharged **neutrons**. Electrons carry a negative charge.

- **Atomic number:** the number of protons. This identifies the element.
- **Mass number:** the total number of protons and neutrons.
- **Isotope:** an atom of the same element with a different number of neutrons.

## A useful example

Carbon always has six protons. Carbon-12 has six neutrons, while carbon-14 has eight. They are isotopes of the same element.

> Changing the number of protons changes the element. Changing the number of neutrons changes the isotope.

# From atoms to substances

## Elements and compounds

An **element** contains only one type of atom. A **compound** contains atoms of two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio.

Water is a compound: each molecule contains two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, written as `H₂O`.

## Mixtures

A mixture contains substances that are physically combined. Their proportions can vary. Air is a mixture of gases; salt water is a mixture of salt and water.

## Check your understanding

1. Which particle determines an element's identity?
2. How does carbon-14 differ from carbon-12?
3. Why is water a compound while air is a mixture?

**Answers:** Protons; two additional neutrons; water has a fixed chemical composition, while air contains physically mixed gases in variable proportions.
